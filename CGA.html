About us

CGA was founded on a simple belief: farmers are the backbone of this country, and they deserve tools that match the seriousness of the work they do. We build soil intelligence software that turns a handful of dirt into a clear, actionable picture — pH, nutrients, texture, and a plain-language recommendation, without a lab technician or a degree in agronomy required.

What we do

Every grower who joins completes a short onboarding questionnaire about their crop, soil, and climate. From there, CGA generates a CGA Result: a full soil health snapshot covering macronutrients (N-P-K), micronutrients, secondary nutrients, organic matter, cation exchange capacity, and soil texture — plus one clear recommendation for what to do next.

Our approach

We favor plain language over jargon, one clear recommendation over a wall of numbers, and steady, incremental improvement over guesswork. This demo generates readings from your onboarding answers as a working preview of the product; the same engine is built to plug directly into real soil sensors and lab reports as our sensor network expands.

Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026. This explains, in plain language, what CGA collects and how it's used.

What we collect

When you create an account, we ask for your email address, phone number, and a password. When you complete onboarding, we ask about your crop, soil, and climate so we can tailor your CGA Result.

How this demo stores data

This preview runs entirely in your browser tab: everything you enter is held in memory for the session only and is never sent to a server or stored permanently. Refreshing the page or closing the tab clears it. Our production system stores accounts and readings in an encrypted database instead.

How we'd use it in production

Account details are used only to run your account — log you in, save your onboarding answers, and show your soil history — never sold to advertisers or third parties. Crop and soil answers are used to generate your CGA Result and improve our recommendation engine.

How to get accurate soil test results

  • 1Take multiple samples from different spots in the field and mix them together.
  • 2Avoid unusual areas — near roads, compost piles, fences, or animal droppings.
  • 3Sample 6–8 inches deep for most gardens and row crops.
  • 4Use clean tools each time to avoid cross-contamination between samples.
